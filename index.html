Where Economics Meets Geography

Hello! I'm a recent UC Santa Barbara graduate with a double major in Economics and Geography, emphasis in GIS. My work sits at the intersection of spatial analysis, economic systems, and emerging technology.

As a GIS analyst with experience in remote sensing, LiDAR data, and spatial modeling, I am fascinated by how spatial thinking can be applied to business and development for improved efficiency, growth, and site selection. I currently work part-time as a GIS & Cartography Subcontractor for Barefoot Earthworks, and I hold an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot License with hands-on experience operating drones for research and content creation.

I'm exploring how geospatial intelligence, real estate analytics, and drone technology converge, and I'm looking for opportunities to put that to work. If you need someone who understands the spatial dimension of investment as well as the financial side, I am the perfect candidate for your role. In the real estate investment and development world, these roles tend to be separate, with geospatial professionals and financial modelers speaking different languages. I aspire to bridge that gap and synergize these roles — from underwriting an 8-unit investment property with a full pro forma to running multi-criteria suitability models for pumped hydroelectric siting across California.

Pumped Hydro Site Suitability Modeling

Ran a multi-criteria GIS suitability and risk analysis to identify the best sites for pumped hydroelectric energy storage paired with solar generation across Orange, Imperial, Riverside, and San Diego counties. LA 2028 Olympics Transit Access

Analyzed walking, bus, and metro accessibility to LA 2028 Olympic venues using census, hotel, and transit stop data. Used Local Moran's I spatial autocorrelation and regression to flag hotel clusters with poor public transit access, informing recommendations for bus route expansion ahead of the Games. Found that roughly 1.18 million residents have adequate transit or walking access to a venue.

Glacial Flood Risk Mapping, Alaska

Investigated whether satellite-based water-index analysis (NDWI) can predict Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) events near Juneau, Alaska before they happen. Combined NDWI change detection with a DEM of the basin to track two 2024 flood events, finding that satellite detection preceded peak discharge by hours ‚Äî a promising signal for early warning systems in hard-to-monitor terrain.
